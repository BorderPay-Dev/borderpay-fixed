# BorderPay Proposal
## Payout Corridor Expansion: Benin, Botswana, Mozambique
**Date:** July 3, 2026  
**Prepared for:** Breedj (Nicolas Goldstein)  
**Prepared by:** BorderPay Africa, Inc.

## 1) Executive Summary
Breedj needs reliable payout coverage in Benin, Botswana, and Mozambique where current rails are constrained. BorderPay proposes a phased corridor rollout designed to outperform traditional banking operations on speed, reconciliation quality, and payout success while maintaining compliance-first controls.

This proposal is structured to avoid overpromising unavailable rails and to deliver measurable outcomes fast through a controlled pilot.

## 2) Problem Statement
- Current payout operations face corridor gaps in Benin, Botswana, and Mozambique.
- Traditional cross-border bank workflows create operational friction:
  - slower settlement windows,
  - higher exception handling burden,
  - weaker payout observability and reconciliation.
- Talent payout programs need predictable, auditable, and scalable disbursement operations.

## 3) Proposed Solution Model
BorderPay will deploy a corridor-by-corridor payout model with:
- destination-specific rail routing,
- pre-flight recipient validation controls,
- end-to-end payout lifecycle tracking,
- centralized reconciliation and operations monitoring.

The model is intentionally phased:
- **Phase A (Design + Validation):** corridor eligibility, compliance checks, payout method selection.
- **Phase B (Pilot):** controlled payout batches in each market.
- **Phase C (Scale):** production volume ramp with SLA governance and exception handling.

## 4) Target Outcomes
- Reduce payout operational overhead compared to ad hoc bank-only processing.
- Improve payout predictability (status visibility, retries, and exception workflows).
- Improve reconciliation quality for finance and operations teams.
- Establish a repeatable rollout playbook for additional African corridors.

## 5) Scope: Benin, Botswana, Mozambique
### In Scope
- corridor readiness validation,
- recipient payout flow mapping,
- payout execution pilot,
- reporting and reconciliation workflow.

### Out of Scope (until explicitly validated)
- claiming unsupported local rail capabilities before readiness confirmation,
- production-wide rollout without pilot evidence.

## 6) Delivery Plan
### Step 1: Corridor Assessment (Fast Start)
Inputs required from Breedj:
- monthly payout volume by country,
- average ticket size,
- payout frequency,
- recipient profile (individual/business),
- expected settlement SLA.

Outputs from BorderPay:
- corridor capability matrix,
- operational risk notes,
- recommended payout path per country.

### Step 2: Commercial + Operations Benchmark
- Compare existing payout baseline against proposed corridor model.
- Define expected cost/service envelope by transfer bands.
- Confirm exception handling and support model.

### Step 3: Pilot Execution
- Execute controlled payout batches in each target market.
- Track:
  - success rate,
  - settlement behavior,
  - failure reasons,
  - reconciliation latency.

### Step 4: Production Scale
- Expand to agreed volume thresholds.
- Add monthly performance governance:
  - payout success metrics,
  - settlement distribution,
  - incident/retry trends.

## 7) Commercial Framework (Proposal Basis)
Commercials should be finalized after Step 1 data intake. Initial framework:
- transparent per-transfer fee policy by payout band,
- no hidden line-item charges,
- monthly reporting pack with corridor-level metrics.

## 8) Compliance and Controls
- Compliance-first onboarding and payout controls per corridor.
- Event-level traceability for payout lifecycle events.
- Controlled retry and failure handling to prevent duplicate disbursements.
